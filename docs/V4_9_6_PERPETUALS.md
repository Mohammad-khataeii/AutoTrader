# V4.9.6 · BTC perpetuals paper lab

## Application review and design

The official and personal paper engines trade EUR spot through Revolut X public data, spot inventory, cash reservations, a spot execution gateway and persistent accounting. Existing shadow ledgers deliberately pin the execution source and strategy manifests. The worker also publishes signals, feeds isolated experiments, and maintains spot protections; research and ML collection have their own paths and authority gates. The React dashboard consumes authenticated FastAPI projections. Staging uses named PostgreSQL/Redis volumes and versioned Compose releases.

Perpetuals need short positions, segregated collateral, mark-price protection, and funding on a separate clock. Extending the EUR broker would change existing spot assumptions and its pinned execution fingerprint. The new lab therefore has its own Compose worker and PostgreSQL tables, its own Alembic revision stream, an authenticated API, and a dashboard tab. Its tables share the staging database backup but do not use the spot journal, EUR capital, order gateways, approved strategy list, shadow databases, or ML. The worker has no exchange credentials or order routes. The lab is opt-in in local/staging paper mode; once provisioned it starts with entries paused. Pause entries in the app while positions are open so the worker can continue protecting them.

## Research and strategy choice

| Candidate | What the original evidence supports | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directional trend / breakout | [Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/mosk.pdf) document time-series momentum across futures, mainly over months. [Liu & Tsyvinski](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24877) find crypto time-series momentum in their historical sample. [Wen et al.](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum/) find conditional intraday predictability in Bitcoin. These are reasons to investigate, not validation of this venue, horizon, or threshold. | First lab hypothesis: simple symmetric long/short rules, frozen across the three accounts. |
| Short-horizon mean reversion | [Wen et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1062940822000833) document both continuation and reversal, with liquidity and jumps affecting the result. [Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439) show why rebounds are a separate stress regime for momentum strategies. | Later comparison with predefined range and volatility filters. Do not add dip buying, averaging down, or recovery multipliers here. |
| Funding or basis carry | The [BIS crypto carry study](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry) links basis variation to leveraged demand and capital constraints; its spot/futures cash-and-carry example still faces margin and liquidation risk. Positive perpetual funding on its own does not hedge directional exposure or guarantee the next payment. | Book observed funding as a cost/credit. Defer a carry strategy until both legs, their fills, transfers, funding and liquidation can be modeled together. |
| Cross-sectional momentum | [Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13119) study a cross-section of coins. Trading only BTC cannot reproduce that factor. | Defer until multiple instruments have clean, aligned histories, with delistings, liquidity and correlated margin considered. |

The registered rule evaluates completed 15-minute candles. A close beyond the preceding 20-bar high or low must agree with the direction of the 8-bar versus 24-bar mean. Stop distance is twice the 14-bar average true range with a 1% floor; skip above 3%. The profit target is twice that price distance; the time limit is six hours. There is at most one open position per account. A signal records an intent before the eligible minute opens. Old signals and missed entries are not backfilled. Existing positions replay contiguous closed minutes after restart.

Each account starts with **1,000 simulated USDT**. **1× / 3× / 10×** divide entry notional to get isolated margin. A 1,000 USDT position would therefore require 100 USDT of initial margin at 10× before fees. A 1% adverse move on that notional is 10 USDT before costs, or 10% of that margin. The app actually sizes by a 0.5% planned account loss budget including modeled execution costs, with caps on collateral and notional; gaps can exceed planned loss. Identical risk caps can produce identical notionals in different cohorts, making their collateral and liquidation differences easier to compare. Each cohort stops taking entries at a 10% drawdown from its observed equity peak.

## Market and accounting conventions

- Uses Bybit's public [trade candles](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/kline), [mark candles](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/mark-kline), [instrument metadata](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/instrument), and [settled funding history](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/history-fund-rate). It checks the BTCUSDT linear contract and funding interval, validates timestamps and prices, and requires aligned, closed minutes. Missing data holds the cursor and blocks new fills; stale values are labeled in the UI. A persistent missing minute requires operator investigation before processing can resume.
- Trading fees (6 basis points each way), slippage (5 basis points each way), and maintenance margin (0.5% plus a close fee allowance) are fixed **model inputs**. They do not represent a Bybit account tier or executable quotes. Entry size uses venue quantity increments and minimums. Entry skips large price gaps or a material trade/mark divergence.
- Following the venue's [isolated liquidation convention](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/UTA-Trading-Rules-Liquidation-Process), the mark triggers liquidation. The lab also uses mark for protective stops. Simultaneous triggers are resolved in the order liquidation, stop, target, time exit. Stop uses an adverse trade-price proxy; the target must be penetrated on the trade candle. The model forfeits the isolated margin on liquidation and does not take extra cash from the account. Intraminute order, book depth, partial liquidations, venue insurance, and exchange fees/tiers are not modeled.
- Following [Bybit's funding convention](https://www.bybit.com/en/contract-rules), positive funding is charged to longs and credited to shorts. The lab settles only positions already open at the event, at the minute's opening mark proxy. Funding is kept in isolated collateral, where it can change the liquidation threshold. It uses BTC's declared eight-hour schedule and fails closed when a required event is missing. Venue timing, interval changes, and endpoint access from Aruba have not been verified by runtime testing.
- State and audit events commit in one transaction with a generation check so concurrent cycles or entry-control changes cannot post duplicate fills. Balances are independently reconciled to closed P&L and locked collateral. The Aruba backup verifier includes and seals the lab tables when present.

## Evaluation plan for a later approved validation

1. Establish causal replay using aligned minute trade/mark data, settled funding and current instrument metadata. Prove long/short cash conservation, signed funding, min-size skips, gap loss, liquidation ordering, no duplicate posts after restart, pause/expiry, rollback and isolated restore.
2. Freeze parameters before chronological walk-forward. Compare no trade, the 1× control, 3× and 10×, and a fixed-notional variant to isolate leverage from sizing. Separate long and short contributions and report upside/downside trends, range, high volatility and rebound periods. Check that a result is not concentrated in a handful of trades.
3. Stress wider costs, funding spikes, provider gaps and delayed entries. Report net P&L, drawdown, margin utilization, liquidation count, skipped fills, turnover, fee/funding drag, and uncertainty. Capture prospective paper results separately after a validated deployment. No validation or profitability claim is made for this candidate.
